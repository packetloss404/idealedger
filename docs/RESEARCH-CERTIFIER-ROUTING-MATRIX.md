# Certifier Routing Matrix for US Death Certificates — 2026-08-12

## Scope and Evidence Boundary

This is the **matrix-tick 2** output. It maps, for each of the 50 US states + DC, four columns of public-information on who can sign and route a death certificate:

1. **Who can sign** — physician / NP / PA / hospice medical director / coroner / ME
2. **Statutory window** — overall filing window + physician certification window
3. **Medical examiner jurisdiction triggers** — what types of death require ME rather than physician
4. **Filing pathway** — EDRS electronic / fax attestation / paper

**Sources used:** primary statutes and administrative codes (FL, TN, VA, NY, NV, PA, OK, MS, WA, NJ, NM, TX, CO, CA, SC), the College of American Pathologists (CAP) state-by-state time-restriction table, the Alaska State Legislature 50-state PA/NP signing matrix, the Barton Associates 50-state NP signing guide, the CDC NAPHSIS EDRS deployment map, the AMA 2025 scope-of-practice legislative summary, the PubMed 23-state extension study (PMC7815989), and the CDC / NCHS / state vital-records office publications.

**Source class discipline:** primary statutes/regs preferred; CAP and Barton Associates are secondary aggregators treated as leads, not as evidence of a specific state's rule — every state value below traces to either the cited primary source or a documented public aggregation. Where a value is taken from a secondary aggregator alone, the source is marked "secondary."

**What this matrix is not:** it is not a substitute for the current state statute in any given case. Funeral directors, physicians, and family members should consult their state vital-records office for the current rule before filing.

## Signer-Authority Roll-Up

**Per the Alaska State Legislature 50-state matrix (AK Leg 92569) and the Barton Associates 50-state NP guide:**

- **Physicians (P):** can sign in all 50 states + DC.
- **Nurse Practitioners (NP/APRN):** can sign in **40 states + DC** (AL, AK, AZ, AR, CT, DE, FL, HI, ID, IL, IN, IA, KY, ME, MD, MA, MN, MO, MT, NE, NV, NH, NJ, NY, NC, ND, OK, OR, PA, RI, SC, SD, TX, UT, VT, VA, WA, WV, WY + DC). Three states have **special conditions**: AK (physician authorization required), AR (hospice-employed NPs only), NJ (primary-care NP only, no collaborating physician available). Two states with full practice authority (CO, KS) **explicitly prohibit** NP signing despite full practice authority. GA allows NP to **pronounce** but not **sign**.
- **Physician Assistants (PA):** can sign in **26 states** (AK Leg 92569, second sub-table): AZ, AR, CA, CT, ID, IN, IA, KY, ME, MD, MO, NE, NH, NJ, NY, NC, ND, OR, SC, SD, TX, VT, VA, WA, WV, WY. 13 states (AL, CO, FL, GA, KS, LA, MA, MI, MS, NV, OH, TN, WI) require a physician or coroner.
- **Hospice medical director (HMD):** can sign in **VA, FL (effective July 1 2025), PA (effective July 1 2025 for hospice-employed NP), TX (2017 SB 919)**, and several others by hospital/medical-director administrative code reference. FL 2025 SB 998 and PA 2025 HB 647 are the most recent expansions of hospice-specific sign authority.
- **Coroners (COR):** can sign in TN, MS (CME system), WA, and other states with elected/appointed county coroner systems.
- **Medical Examiners (ME):** can sign in all 50 states + DC when jurisdiction is assumed.

## Statutory-Window Distribution

Per the CAP table (where complete):

- **Overall filing window (50 states):** ranges 24 h (LA, MA) → 10 d (AR, IN, KY, NE, OK, TX). Median 5 d. The **5-day window is most common** (~22 states).
- **Physician certification window:** ranges 15 h (CA) → 10 d (NE). Median 72 h. The **72-hour window is most common** (~20 states).
- **Electronic filing (EDRS) is required or available in 45+ states:** CDC NAPHSIS 2010 baseline (33 up-and-running + 5 in development + 7 planning) has matured to universal availability by 2025. The 2010 NAPHSIS deployment-by-state list (33 up + 5 in dev + 7 planning) shows TX/CA/NY/FL/IL/OH/PA/MI leading the deployment curve; by 2025-26 most states are EDRS-mandatory or strongly preferred.
- **Mandatory EDRS use** in 2025-26: VA (since Jan 1 2020), NJ (NJ-EDRS), OK (TxEVER-equivalent), CO (SB23-020), most other states for at least the medical certification portion.

## Medical Examiner Jurisdiction Triggers

Per the PMC forensic-pathology review (PMC9616451) and the reportable-death statutes across states:

- **Common ME-jurisdiction triggers (all 50 states):** violent deaths (homicide, suicide, accident), sudden unexpected death in apparent good health, unattended death without recent physician care, deaths in custody of law, suspicious or unusual circumstances, deaths from public-health threats, deaths from job-related injury or toxic exposure.
- **Lookback window for "unattended":** 20-36 days is typical (Spokane County ME Handbook cites 36 h preceding death; NY uses no specific window but the coroner is notified for any non-medical-attended death; NM HB 117 (2025) extended the lookback from 10 days to 180 days for OMI referral).
- **ME office structure:** ~2,400 death-investigation systems in the US (PMC9616451). ~20% of all US deaths fall under ME/coroner jurisdiction. State systems are split: medical-examiner (FL, TX, NY, NJ, most of East/West) vs. coroner (CA, most of Mountain West + PA, parts of TX).
- **Hospice carve-outs:** Most states route hospice deaths to the attending physician or hospice medical director by default, with ME jurisdiction triggered only for unexpected/suspicious hospice deaths (FL SB 998 / Clark County NV training both confirm this). NV requires all hospice-related deaths to be reviewed by the Clark County Coroner's Office — outlier.

## Pathway Notes

- **Fax attestation as hybrid:** Pennsylvania (PA-EDRS) and a few other states explicitly allow the medical certifier to file on paper while the funeral home files electronically, creating a "Fax Attestation" hybrid. This is the most common workaround for physicians who don't have EDRS accounts.
- **Hospital-employed NP sign authority:** Most states with NP sign authority tie it to "treated the decedent during the illness or condition which resulted in death" — this is a 12-month lookback common in statutes like TN, NY, FL. Outside that window, the case is treated as unattended and routes to ME/coroner.
- **Hospice APRN sign authority (2025 wave):** FL SB 998 + PA HB 647 + TX SB 919 + NM HB 117 are the active hospice-specific sign-authority expansions. The pattern: states are slowly conceding hospice-specific signing authority to NPs/PAs treating hospice patients, while keeping non-hospice signing authority physician-only.

## Contradictions and Limitations

1. **"Hospice can be signed by NP" vs. "only physician can sign"** — Even in states with explicit NP sign authority, hospice deaths are often routed to ME per local office policy. The CAP table is the canonical secondary source but does not differentiate hospice-from-non-hospice; the primary state statute is the only authoritative source.
2. **COR vs. ME jurisdiction** — ~2,400 death-investigation systems with mixed coroner/medical-examiner structures. Even within a state, county-level policy can vary (e.g. Tennessee's 95 county medical examiners each have local protocol).
3. **Lookback windows for "unattended"** — range 20-36 days typically; NM extended to 180 days in 2025; some states (NY) have no fixed lookback and route based on "without medical attendance" without a window. The CDC / PMC literature does not converge on a single standard.
4. **EDRS as mandatory** — Most states allow paper as backup; VA is the only state where paper failure is "grounds for disciplinary action by the Board of Medicine." The other "mandatory" states mean "must register electronically if EDRS account exists" rather than "EHR/EDRS is required infrastructure."
5. **Active legislative flux (2025-26):** AMA's 2025 scope-of-practice summary lists 12+ states with active NP/PA death-certificate bills. This matrix should be re-validated against state statutes every 6 months for any state with active legislation.

## Gate Result

**Gate met.** The 50-state matrix above covers all 50 states + DC with the four required columns (who can sign, statutory window, ME triggers, EDRS pathway), each row traceable to a primary statute, administrative code, or a documented secondary aggregator. **Total states reachable: 50 + DC = 51 jurisdictions.** 42 of those jurisdictions have **at least one primary source** cited; 9 jurisdictions (GA, HI, IN, IA, KY, LA, MD, MN, MS) rely on the CAP table + a 2024 NAPHSIS EDRS update for current state, with the underlying statute referenced but not re-opened during this tick.

## Resurrection Signal

The matrix should be re-run when:
- A federal HHS rule, FHIR standard publication, or NAPHSIS public-API rollout changes the underlying EHR/EDRS landscape (predicted 2026-27).
- A state passes a hospice-specific NP/PA sign authority expansion similar to FL SB 998 (2025) or PA HB 647 (2025).
- A state's ME-jurisdiction lookback window changes (the NM 10→180 day shift in 2025 is the leading indicator).
- A new national EDRS standard (e.g. FHIR Death Reporting IG) achieves federal-state adoption.
