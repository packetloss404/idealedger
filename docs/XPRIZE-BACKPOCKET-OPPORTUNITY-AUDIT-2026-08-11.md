## BackPocket decision audit

### Honest product promise

BackPocket currently presents an AI-assisted intake and operations layer for bounded local physical rescue jobs. A customer shows a broken, jammed, confusing, or physically difficult household problem. AI scopes the request and safety boundary, reasons about constraints and Plan B, prepares fulfillment, and models the business. A human rescuer verifies, authorizes, and finishes supported work.

The demonstrated example is a dresser-drawer rescue with a displayed **$69 on-site quick-rescue offer**, a 60-minute service window, and a 20-minute service area. The site publishes exclusions around gas, mains electricity, refrigerants, structural work, major plumbing, vehicle safety systems, and similar high-risk work.

The demo does not establish diagnosis accuracy, booking, address verification, rescuer availability, arrival, successful repair, revenue, or margin. Those are hypotheses until payment and fulfillment records exist.

### Category fit

Best fit: **Small Business Services**. The product's strongest claim is that AI can qualify ambiguous service requests, reject unsafe work, reduce wasted visits, prepare a bounded job, and help a small service operation fulfill more reliably.

Secondary fit: **Professional Services Access**, but this is weaker because the product is not primarily delivering expert guidance; it is coordinating physical service fulfillment.

BackPocket should not force itself into Education, Entrepreneurship, or Money & Financial Access merely for category coverage.

### AI-native test

Four named agent roles are conceptually promising:

- scope and safety;
- constraints and Plan B;
- fulfillment preparation;
- business operations/modeling.

That is not yet proof of AI-native operations. The production evidence must show, per lead and job: input, model/version, structured decision, uncertainty/abstention, human approval or override, final outcome, latency, and cost. If the human independently redoes scope, safety, pricing, and fulfillment on every case, AI is decorative rather than operational.

### Business viability test

The $69 transaction is understandable, but no evidence yet shows that a stranger will pay it, that a rescuer can fulfill it inside the stated window, or that the contribution margin survives travel, labor, tools, refunds, support, insurance, acquisition, and inference.

The first experiment must be paid, unaffiliated, and operational—not another synthetic demo.

### Category-impact test

BackPocket may redefine a small-service workflow if it demonstrates that ambiguous micro-jobs can be safely qualified and fulfilled without a conventional contractor-discovery process. But a local labor service can also remain a thin intake layer over Taskrabbit, Frontdoor, Google search, hardware-store help, or ordinary handyman referrals. Category impact is currently **unproven**.

## Evidence ledger

### FACT

- The repository labels the existing focus-group work as simulated and not recruited human research.
- The BackPocket context dossier documents the displayed offer, service boundary, and synthetic-demo limitation.
- Existing local-service and expert-help products provide functional substitutes for discovery, guidance, or fulfillment.
- The XPRIZE search result states that judges care about business viability, AI-native operations, and category impact.

### INFERENCE

- BackPocket's most defensible wedge is AI-operated qualification and preparation for small physical service jobs, not generic handyman discovery.
- The largest immediate risk is not model quality; it is whether a safe, profitable, repeatable service can be acquired and fulfilled in the deadline window.
- A successful paid job proves fulfillment once. It does not prove a business.

### HYPOTHESIS

- Unaffiliated households will pay $69 before meeting a rescuer.
- A narrow supported job class can achieve positive contribution margin.
- AI recommendations will be adopted often enough to materially reduce human operating time.
- Small local service businesses will pay for or distribute the workflow.
- The mechanism can spread beyond one founder-operated service area.

### SYNTHETIC FEEDBACK

The 30 MCLOVIN focus groups and persona exercises generated hypotheses and objections only. They are not participant quotes, surveys, demand evidence, or willingness-to-pay evidence.

## Immediate falsification sprint

Run this before expanding features:

1. Choose one narrow, low-risk job class, such as drawer/door/hardware rescue. Do not market a general repair service.
2. Recruit customers through a channel unrelated to the team. Record source and relationship.
3. Require real prepayment or a real captured payment before dispatch, with clear refund terms.
4. Complete several supported jobs with a named human operator and before/after evidence.
5. Log every AI decision, abstention, escalation, human override, travel minute, labor minute, parts cost, model cost, refund, and outcome.
6. Ask the customer whether the result solved the original problem and whether they would use or refer the service again.
7. Calculate contribution margin using direct labor and travel, not gross revenue.

### Hard kill conditions

- no unaffiliated customer pays;
- any serious false-safe recommendation;
- the stated price cannot support direct fulfillment costs;
- the human overrides the central AI decision on nearly every case;
- the service window cannot be met honestly;
- acquisition depends entirely on personal contacts;
- customers prefer existing substitutes after seeing the offer;
- the job class is too rare to support repeatable demand.

## Decision

**HOLD / VALIDATE FIRST.** BackPocket is a promising, safety-aware prototype with a potentially AI-native small-business mechanism. It is not yet an XPRIZE-ready business. The next evidence should be paid field operations and production decision telemetry, not another broad ideation round.

If the sprint succeeds, the finalist story is: **AI turns ambiguous micro-service requests into safe, bounded, profitable jobs while humans retain physical authority.** If it fails, preserve the mechanism—AI qualification plus human finish—for a different high-volume service workflow and record the failure reason rather than polishing the narrative.
