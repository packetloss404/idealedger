## Round 12 verdict

**Final follow-up (2026-08-11):** SnapInvoice is now **passed_market**. Outreach could not recruit even three eligible operators, so the acquisition/distribution gate failed before deposits, fixtures, or technical validation. LotMatch returns to the front of the funded experiment queue.

1. **The user-instrumentable "process tracker" hypothesis (H1) is FALSIFIED.** The literal cell — consumer-owned, multi-provider, user-instrumented status tracking with no provider state — is genuinely empty, and it is empty for economic reasons, not oversight: consumers demonstrably pay only for *actual* provider status (Lawfully, 6M+ cases, proves willingness to pay for real status only); episodic processes kill the between-process ritual; and the shareable status-page mechanism is already commoditized from the business side at the price floor (TrueStatus, BuildStatus at $15/mo, SiteUpdate/Handoff, Jobber/Buildertrend). This is a third independent confirmation of the Round-11 rule: **empty cells are usually empty for demand/economics reasons.**
2. **SnapInvoice survives only as a narrower behavior hypothesis.** The original sweep found no obvious standalone match for batch job-photos → invoice lines + un-invoiced detector + inbound-share intake. The post-commit primary-source review found SimplyWise already combining photo-to-line-item estimates with invoicing, plus BldrOS, PeakPro, PlanMyCrew, SodaDesk, and TidyShift on adjacent photo-backed billing or completed-but-unbilled detection. The no-suite forwarding interaction may remain open, but novelty is now **at most 40/100** and Gate G0 must prove paid rejection of those suites before any fixture or build.
3. **The Weirdness Round (42 divergent ideas) produced no new BUILD** — re-confirming the B2B clone wave. One durable mechanism survived: the **host-buys-transparency inversion** (make the host the customer to defeat host-owns-correction), already partly occupied by CompanyCam/Timemark "Photo Proof for Work" but with an open wedge in the standalone solo-shop customer-facing sign-off.
4. **The mechanism map is the round's most important learning:** the dead concepts' mechanisms were almost never wrong — **the payer assignment was the bug.** Camera-evidence, photo-OCR, deadline-clock, and reconciliation mechanisms all work; they died on who pays and who owns the correction. The winning move is to take a working loser-mechanism and point it at a business that already has a weekly money flow and owns its own correction step. SnapInvoice is the best testable instance from this round, but its outcome-level crowding means payer alignment alone is insufficient; the no-suite behavior must earn payment.

## New third candidate: Freight-Line Pro (freight photo-class for weekly pro-shippers)

- User: auction houses, estate liquidators, and furniture dealers who ship multiple items weekly.
- Pain: freight-class and DIM-weight misclassification eats margin; shipping cost surprises kill deals (the large-item shipping evidence from R11; the reseller/ShipKit cluster).
- Mechanic transplant: camera measurement → freight class → live quote → claim-proof packaging photos, built as a weekly-volume margin tool for businesses that already ship every week (vs ShipKit's individual resellers).
- Why now: phone camera + freight APIs (uShip/LTL rails) + on-device structuring; no phone-native photo→class→quote→claim kit found for pro-shippers.
- Status: **raw** — pain and payer are plausible but the competitor check is incomplete (search engines bot-walled mid-pass; "unverified-occupied" not "verified-empty"). Cheap test: 5 auction/estate-liquidation businesses, 20 shipments each, measure misclassification recoveries.
- Note: partially overlaps parked ShipKit; keep separate because the payer (business with weekly volume) is materially different from the reseller.

## Weirdness-Round survivors (all failed build screening, recorded for mechanism value)

- **Done ≠ Confirmed** — sell the repair shop a verified-done transparency tool (the shop pays). PARTIAL to CompanyCam/Timemark; wedge open but the buyer is a shop already paying for suites.
- **Claim-Ready Install Record** — sell contractors a claim-ready install file (serial + date + photos) built at origin so home-warranty "no records" denials cannot be made. Host-side absorption risk (Liberty Home Guard, AHS capture model numbers); vendor-neutral portable claim file open.

## Falsification design for SnapInvoice (the executable output)

See `docs/ROUND-12-SNAPINVOICE-BUILD-SPEC.md`. Summary: Gate G0 first requires seven of ten eligible ops to demonstrate why the nearby suites do not fit and four to place refundable deposits. Only then run G1 extraction accuracy + zero fabricated lines, G2 ≥7/10 ops forwarding ≥80% of real photos for three weeks, and G3 ≥4/10 paid founder conversions. The likely failure is demand for the narrow behavior, followed by adherence—not OCR.

## New / refined heuristics

1. **Mechanisms survive; payer assignments die.** When a concept fails, separate the mechanism from the payer/correction-owner. The mechanism is often reusable; the payer assignment was the bug. Confidence: **High** (across ~18 mechanisms in the R12 map).
2. **The host-buys-transparency inversion** is a legitimate escape from host-owns-correction: if the host is the customer, the app can own the correction step. Apply when a host-owns-correction kill otherwise blocks a real pain. Confidence: **Medium** (CompanyCam proves the model; wedge crowding is the risk).
3. **"No competitor" cells are empty for demand/economics reasons unless a fresh mechanism transplant explains the emptiness.** The process-tracker cell looked empty and was economically dead; the photo→invoice interaction looked open but the desired outcome is already served by a fast-moving suite swarm. A business payer with weekly money flow is necessary, not sufficient. Confidence: **High**.
4. **Consumer willingness to pay is for actual provider status, not for one's own record.** Do not build user-instrumented "my own truth" products for consumers; business-side instrumented truth (contractor/status-page) is where the payer is. Confidence: **High** (Lawfully + TrueStatus/BuildStatus + R11 evidence).
5. **Share-sheet / forwarding intake is a durable differentiator against suites** that demand open-app-create-job; it is the behavior contract that makes micro-ops adopt. Confidence: **Medium** (R11 heuristic 5 reinforced by the R12 adherence-risk analysis).
