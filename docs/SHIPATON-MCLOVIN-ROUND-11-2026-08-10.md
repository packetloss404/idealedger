# Shipaton MCLOVIN Research Round 11 — 2026-08-10

## Decision

**No candidate earns `BUILD`.** Only one idea remains a finalist, and even it is `VALIDATE FIRST`. The bounded work is ranked by the quality of the next falsification test rather than by prose or demo appeal:

1. **LotMatch** — real carton fixtures plus live crew adherence.
2. **WeedCheck** — a technical/market spike only: blind physical-fixture accuracy and active-HTV-seller use.
3. **DoorHold** — a manual incidence and ordinary-SMS comparison only.
4. **Monetization Canary** — `HOLD` behind RevenueCat roadmap and paid-demand gates.

BackPocket remains the active XPRIZE build, but it is a separate **AI-operated, human-finished local rescue-service prototype**, not a save-and-retrieve app. Its business evidence remains **QUARANTINE/HOLD** until real paid jobs, safe fulfillment, agent decision logs, and unit economics exist. Change Impact Relay, Client Decision Debt Clock, and Did-It-Fix-It leave the active shortlist after exact-current competitors and substitutes were found. Zero builds is preferable to turning simulated feedback and desk research into false demand.

## Epistemic boundary

- **FACT** is supported directly by a cited source or canonical repository record.
- **INFERENCE** interprets one or more facts.
- **HYPOTHESIS** remains unproven and has a falsification gate.
- **SYNTHETIC FEEDBACK** came from simulated constituencies. It is not a customer quote, interview, survey, or willingness-to-pay signal.
- Product pages prove that a workflow is offered; they do not prove adoption, effectiveness, or satisfaction.
- Forum and review evidence is directional. It establishes that a complaint exists, not its prevalence.
- Scores are decision aids, not measured precision. A hard kill gate overrides a high average.

### Recurring reasons ideas died

1. The phone did not own or receive authoritative state.
2. A manual ritual was required at a rare event.
3. The product implied certainty unavailable from its observations.
4. Two records lacked a stable timing, identity, aggregation, or physical invariant.
5. A strong incumbent, free native feature, or host system already owned correction.
6. Acknowledgment was stretched into identity, custody, payment, completion, safety, or legal proof.
7. Two-party onboarding or a marketplace was required before solo value.
8. Frequency, retention, willingness to pay, or acquisition failed.
9. The concept was another camera + reminder + PDF, timer, generic tracker, wrapper, or dashboard.
10. The demo was striking but the recurring business was not.

### Ten prior lessons binding this round

1. No owned or sanctioned state, no truthful product.
2. Authority is necessary but insufficient when a cheap focused utility already owns the action.
3. An acknowledgment proves only that an account or link holder acknowledged a bounded card.
4. `Unknown`, `unavailable`, `not comparable`, and `not acknowledged` must be first-class states.
5. A false green at a consequential boundary is a kill condition.
6. Two records can be compared only when their invariant is explicit and stable.
7. New rituals must prove real adherence before engineering.
8. When the host owns correction, a phone-side checker is usually a feature.
9. Real fixtures and edge cases come before polished interfaces.
10. Distribution, proprietary data, and honest uncertainty are scarcer than feature ideas.

## Evidence Map

- **Repair outcome:** J.D. Power reported that 12% of dealer-service repairs were not fixed correctly the first time; CARFAX Car Care already provides broad free service-history behavior. See Focus Group 02 sources S04–S06.
- **Family/work coordination:** Pew, ParentSquare, Cozi, Ohai, Homebase, and worker-schedule research validate coordination load while demonstrating incumbent breadth. See Focus Groups 06–10 sources S18–S33.
- **Freelancer delay:** practitioner and trade sources document late payment and administrative friction; HoneyBook and Bonsai show the host-feature threat. See Focus Group 12 sources S39–S43.
- **Accessibility administration:** 2026 wheelchair-repair investigations document weeks- or months-long delays; paratransit records show missed-trip complaints. See Focus Group 16.
- **Indie monetization failures:** RevenueCat’s own current troubleshooting says empty offerings are usually configuration failures; Test Store and debug tooling make preflight easier, while runtime/storefront drift remains possible. See Focus Group 30 and the current [Test Store](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/test-and-launch/sandbox/test-store), [troubleshooting](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/revenuecat-support/general-troubleshooting), and [debugging](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/test-and-launch/debugging) documentation.
- **Contradiction for Monetization Canary:** RevenueCat SDK diagnostics can already send response-time, cache-hit, and error-code telemetry to RevenueCat; RevenueCat Paywalls expose error/not-presented callbacks and encounter charts. PostHog and Sentry can capture custom runtime failures. This makes a separate product a vertical diagnostic preset, not a unique observation capability.
- **BackPocket competition:** the live product competes with local service discovery and fulfillment, not bookmarking. Taskrabbit already lets customers choose, schedule, chat with, pay, and review local workers across furniture assembly, minor repair, moving, cleaning, and related jobs; Frontdoor already combines live expert video guidance with local in-person pros. BackPocket's narrower hypothesis is an AI front office that reasons between an ambiguous physical problem and a prepared bounded human job. [Taskrabbit services](https://www.taskrabbit.com/services), [Frontdoor app](https://www.frontdoor.com/get-the-app).

## Opportunity Map

Three opportunity shapes remained less saturated than their parent categories:

1. **Pair already-occurring events instead of asking for a diary.** Examples: requested artifact ↔ acknowledgment, complaint ↔ stated repair ↔ later observation, paywall attempt ↔ resolved offering ↔ terminal purchase result.
2. **No-install capability links for bounded, low-risk decisions.** This improves DoorHold and Client Decision Debt Clock, but only where link response does not imply identity, payment, legal acceptance, care, or safety.
3. **Exception views instead of another master system.** Consumers often need the one stalled obligation or changed dependency, yet incomplete input can create false calm.

The most interesting transfer was **continuous-integration smoke testing → mobile monetization runtime health**. It is overlooked partly because analytics products optimize conversion after events arrive, while the failure can be that the revenue surface never presented. It may also be overlooked because RevenueCat, Sentry, or PostHog can absorb it cheaply; that ambiguity is the whole falsification test.

### Disruption conclusion

The deeper condition is not a universal “life admin” market. It is **fragmented authority**: the user sees pieces of state but cannot make a truthful unified status because the correcting host is elsewhere. This explains both the frequency of screenshot/text/PDF workarounds and the poor survival rate of consumer coordination apps. The exceptional opportunity must either own an atomic observable boundary, obtain a sanctioned partner event, or reduce its promise to a factual receipt with explicit unknowns.

## Finalist Dossier — LotMatch

### Name

**LotMatch** — working name only.

### One sentence

For flooring and tile crews, when cartons arrive or move onto a job, LotMatch compares the visible product, lot, shade, and caliber tokens with the crew-confirmed baseline, so they no longer have to trust a hurried visual label check before an irreversible installation.

### User and trigger

The primary user is a flooring/tile installer, warehouse lead, or owner. The trigger is already present: a crew checks cartons before opening, staging, mixing, or installing them.

### Existing pain

Mixed product, lot, shade, or caliber can force sorting, rework, visible mismatch, delay, or tear-out. Manufacturer/installer guidance already requires checking labels and physical material. The product must not claim matching cartons are visually compatible or correct for the job; it can only catch a difference among accepted visible label tokens.

### Current solution

Crews compare labels manually, inspect and mix material, consult paperwork, or use warehouse/ERP inventory tools such as QFloors, RFMS, Floorzap, or MeasureSquare. These systems are partial competitors and could add a field comparator where they own the job allocation.

### Product mechanic

1. Scan the first job carton.
2. The app extracts candidate product/lot/shade/caliber fields.
3. The user confirms the exact visible tokens and explicitly marks which fields matter.
4. Scan each subsequent carton before opening/staging.
5. Show `SAME VISIBLE TOKENS`, `MISMATCH`, or `UNREADABLE/REVIEW`; never “safe,” “compatible,” or “correct material.”
6. Preserve the label images and user-confirmed baseline locally for the job.

### Phone advantage

The phone is already at receiving and installation, has a camera, can make repeated scanning faster than transcription, and can provide haptic/audio feedback while hands are occupied. A website would be inferior at the carton boundary.

### Aha moment

The second carton produces an immediate red hold with the differing lot token highlighted before the box is opened.

### Why now

Mobile cameras and on-device OCR can cheaply extract printed tokens, while current flooring software focuses on inventory allocation rather than an independent, atomic field-side comparison. This is an inference, not a durable moat.

### Competitive landscape and novelty claim

No exact standalone on-site carton-label comparator surfaced in the search. Warehouse/ERP products already track lots and scan tags; manual comparison is standard practice. The careful novelty claim is: **a crew-confirmed, label-only mismatch guard at the irreversible jobsite boundary**. It is not a dye-lot oracle, visual matcher, product verifier, or replacement for physical inspection/mixing.

### Monetization

- Free: one active job and a limited number of scans.
- Hypothesis A: $29.99–49.99/year for solo crews/owners with unlimited jobs and export.
- Hypothesis B: job-credit pack for genuinely episodic users.
- RevenueCat: entitlements, trial/intro offer, job-credit consumables if credits win, and transparent paywall measurement.
- Paywall moment: only after the free job has caught a mismatch or completed enough scans to demonstrate saved effort; never before the first useful comparison.

### Retention

The return trigger is each flooring/tile job and each delivery/restock. Retention depends on active crews, not homeowners. The gate is live scan adherence across multiple jobs, not notifications.

### Growth loop

- First 25: direct outreach through local installers/distributors and existing flooring trade communities, with a free real-label test.
- First 100: trade-group demonstrations, distributor counter cards, and short mismatch/unknown videos using real donated labels.
- First 1,000: distributor/ERP partnerships or a repeatable installer-referral channel. Organic app-store discovery alone is not credible.
- Intrinsic sharing: a crew sends the highlighted mismatch to the owner, distributor, or supplier as a factual label comparison, not a referral card.

### Build in Public

Publish the honest fixture corpus: label families, unreadable cases, false-match prevention, field adherence, and every narrowing of the supported scope. The compelling story is the error budget, not an AI demo.

### Shipaton categories

Legitimate fits: Grand only if paid traction appears; HAMM for a well-matched crew plan/job-credit model; Build in Public for the fixture/error log; Design for a fast gloves-on scan flow. Sponsor boxes should not reshape the product.

### Smallest lovable MVP

One platform, tile/flooring cartons only, user-confirmed baseline tokens, repeated exact comparison, local job history, three honest states, haptic/audio result, and a RevenueCat-gated second job. No cloud OCR dependency, visual compatibility judgment, ERP integration, team dashboard, or AI advice.

### Two-minute demo

1. **0–15 seconds:** scan baseline carton; confirm visible product and lot tokens.
2. **15–35:** scan a matching carton; show exact compared fields.
3. **35–55:** scan a planted different-lot carton; red hold and highlighted token.
4. **55–75:** scan glare/damaged label; app refuses certainty with `UNREADABLE/REVIEW`.
5. **75–100:** show local job receipt and send the mismatch image to a supplier.
6. **100–120:** show the narrow claim, fixture results, live adherence, paid users, and what the app explicitly cannot know.

### Technical architecture

- Expo SDK 57 / React Native 0.86 mobile client with camera capture and a custom development build for native on-device text recognition.
- Apple Vision on iOS and ML Kit text recognition on Android behind one conservative token-extraction interface.
- User confirmation creates the authoritative baseline; normalization is limited to casing, whitespace, and explicitly tested punctuation rules.
- Local SQLite job store; images remain on-device by default; export is explicit.
- Every comparison retains observed image, extracted token, normalized token, confidence/quality state, and user correction.
- RevenueCat Purchases for entitlement/job-credit testing.
- No aggregate green state when any required field is missing or unreadable.

### Biggest risk

The workflow owner pays, but a hurried crew performs every scan. If adherence is weak or one false `SAME` appears, the central promise collapses. Even perfect label equality does not prove the material is visually compatible or intended for that job.

### Immediate falsification test

Before UI implementation, collect at least 300 real labels across 10 brands and run blinded pair tests. Require over 99% accepted-token accuracy, **zero false `SAME`**, and `UNREADABLE` for every unsupported/ambiguous case. Then test 10 live crews: over 90% carton adherence, usable target fields on over 90% of cartons, and at least three owner prepays. Kill or narrow on the first failed invariant.

### Score and verdict

Pain 11/12; frequency/consequence 7/8; behavior 7/8; phone 9/10; truth 6/8; novelty 9/12; monetization 7/10; distribution 7/12; retention 4/6; demo 5/5; feasibility 3/5; RevenueCat/category 2/4. **Total 77/100. Verdict: VALIDATE FIRST.**

### Remove-AI test

“Scan each flooring carton and hold any visible product, lot, shade, or caliber token that differs from the crew-confirmed baseline.” The value remains intact without AI language.

### Show-me test

A silent 15-second video shows baseline scan → second scan → one differing printed lot token outlined red → unopened carton moved to a hold area.

### Would somebody text this?

The crew texts the source image and highlighted token difference to the owner or distributor because it is the fastest way to explain why a carton is being held. The share object is intrinsic work evidence, not a referral device.

## LotMatch Failure Pre-mortem

### It is October 2027. LotMatch failed completely.

1. Crews scanned the first few cartons and then stopped under time pressure.
2. Labels varied too much; frequent `UNREADABLE` made manual checking faster.
3. One false `SAME` destroyed trust and exposed the difference between label equality and material compatibility.
4. Flooring ERP vendors added the comparator inside an authoritative job allocation.
5. The market was too narrow and acquisition through trade channels cost more than annual revenue.

### It is October 2027. LotMatch unexpectedly became a $1M/year business.

1. A distributor embedded the flow at receiving and installer pickup, solving distribution and adherence.
2. A real multi-brand label corpus made abstention rare without sacrificing false-match safety.
3. Mismatch shares shortened supplier resolution enough to create measurable ROI.
4. Job credits and team plans matched episodic and high-volume crews better than one forced subscription.
5. The same conservative token gate expanded into adjacent irreversible material workflows without claiming visual compatibility.

### Evidence comparison

The failure narrative is currently better supported: incumbents own allocation, labels vary, and live adherence is unknown. The success narrative depends on a named channel partner, a proprietary fixture corpus, and measurable resolution time. Those are testable, so `VALIDATE FIRST` is justified; `BUILD` is not.

## Lessons learned

### Accepted heuristic 1 — Fragmentation is negative evidence by default

- **Observation:** mature platforms increasingly consolidate the exact fragmentation pitch.
- **Evidence:** Frontdoor, ParentSquare, Homebase, Jobber, ServiceTitan, caregiving suites, RevenueCat, CARFAX, and approval platforms repeatedly absorbed the obvious layer.
- **Proposed heuristic:** do not advance “everything is scattered” unless the product owns a cross-host invariant, has a sanctioned partner event, or attaches at a transaction-timed distribution boundary.
- **Scope:** mature workflow and consumer-administration categories.
- **Counterexample:** a new regulation/API can create portable accepted state.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Accepted heuristic 2 — No-install links are an interaction primitive, not proof

- **Observation:** no-install links reduce two-party adoption in DoorHold, freelance decisions, family reassignment, and handoffs.
- **Evidence:** exact products use magic links successfully, but link response still cannot prove identity, access, payment, custody, care, or physical completion.
- **Proposed heuristic:** prototype low-risk collaboration with a capability link before requiring another app; label the acknowledged proposition exactly.
- **Scope:** bounded choices with low consequence and no strong identity requirement.
- **Counterexample:** medical, financial, legal, safety, or high-value actions requiring authentication.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Accepted heuristic 3 — Search for host observability before building a monitor

- **Observation:** Monetization Canary initially looked like a clean independent opportunity until RevenueCat diagnostics/mobile/debug tooling and generic observability substitutes were mapped.
- **Evidence:** RevenueCat owns offering/purchase telemetry; PostHog/Sentry can accept custom failure events; RevenueCat can expose the vertical alert directly.
- **Proposed heuristic:** a cross-host monitoring idea must first prove the correcting host cannot expose equivalent telemetry and that users will install/pay for another probe.
- **Scope:** developer tools and host-adjacent monitors.
- **Counterexample:** an independent monitor can win when it observes from outside the host or spans vendors with a stable invariant.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Accepted heuristic 4 — Mechanism-level competitor search outranks title novelty

- **Observation:** Client Decision Debt Clock and Change Impact Relay looked title-novel but died to exact-current workflows after searching for magic links, deadline movement, family conflict graphs, and no-app coordination.
- **Evidence:** ApproveEase, Caldzy, Aviva, Ohai, PlanPing, AutoVitals, and the other 2025–2026 products.
- **Proposed heuristic:** search the trigger, interaction, state transition, and outcome separately before recording a novelty claim.
- **Scope:** all app-market novelty research.
- **Counterexample:** a true technical invention may not share product vocabulary.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Proposed heuristic 5 — Service histories under-record outcomes

- **Observation:** home and auto systems record transaction/part/invoice more reliably than whether the original symptom returned.
- **Evidence:** Groups 01–02 and J.D. Power repair evidence.
- **Proposed heuristic:** test complaint → intervention claim → later observation, but never infer causality.
- **Scope:** repeat professional service.
- **Counterexample:** systems with authoritative diagnostic/quality outcome data.
- **Confidence:** Medium; do not promote beyond proposal until partner-distributed behavior is observed.

### Proposed heuristic 6 — Change propagation is a real pain with a dangerous data requirement

- **Observation:** calendars capture events while families/workers recalculate downstream obligations.
- **Evidence:** Groups 05, 07, 08, 10, 12, and 15; exact competitors validate the category.
- **Proposed heuristic:** look for event-relative consequences without requiring a complete persistent dependency graph.
- **Scope:** multi-party schedules.
- **Counterexample:** stable schedules where visibility alone solves the problem.
- **Confidence:** Medium.
