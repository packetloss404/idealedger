# Live Roll concept dossier

**Research date:** August 8, 2026  
**Status:** Conditional go for validation  
**Working name:** Live Roll  
**Recommended direction:** Interactive contact-sheet selection challenges, with live capture as a premiere format

## Executive decision

The original concept was a live contact sheet: a photographer begins a Roll and each shutter progressively adds another still photograph for a remote audience to watch and select.

That horizontal product is not sufficiently differentiated. Capture One Live, Picflow Live, Evoto Instant, Frame.io Camera to Cloud, SpotMyPhotos, Honcho, SnapFlow, PhotoShelter, and event-gallery products already provide most combinations of real-time image delivery, browser viewing, ratings, annotations, client selection, camera tethering, and final delivery.

The differentiated product is the editorial decision itself:

> **Circle the frames you would keep. Lock your edit. Then reveal what the photographer chose, how they finished it, and why.**

The recommended product has two connected formats:

1. **Blind Edit:** An evergreen, archived contact-sheet challenge that can be played at any time.
2. **Live Roll:** An occasional premiere where a finite contact sheet fills while the photographer is shooting, followed by the same independent selection and reveal ritual.

This is not a client gallery, culling utility, photo contest, or generic photo social network. It is an interactive photography-learning and entertainment format built around visual judgment.

The current verdict is **conditional go**, not build approval. It becomes a go only if photographers will license revealing sequences containing outtakes and photography learners repeatedly complete the selection-and-reveal experience.

## Product thesis

Finished photographs hide most of the creative decision.

A contact sheet exposes the approaches, hesitations, variations, misses, and decisive moments that preceded the final image. Photography books, exhibitions, and classroom exercises use contact sheets specifically because the surrounding frames make a photographer's judgment visible. [Magnum Contact Sheets](https://www.thamesandhudson.com/products/magnum-contact-sheets), [Morgan Library: Hujar Contact](https://www.themorgan.org/exhibitions/hujar-contact), and the [Annenberg Contact High educator guide](https://annenbergphotospace.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/ContactHigh_EducatorGuide_final.pdf) are evidence of that educational and cultural interest.

Live Roll turns that normally hidden editorial process into an interaction:

```text
The photographer made 36 frames.
You may circle 5.
The photographer cannot see your circles.
You cannot see the photographer's cut.
Both sides lock.
Then the edits are revealed.
```

The photographer's cut is not treated as an objectively correct answer. The reveal compares intent and taste:

- What did the viewer notice?
- What story was the photographer building?
- Where did the viewer and photographer agree?
- Why did the photographer reject the crowd favorite?
- How did crop, sequencing, and development change the selected frame?

The concise positioning is:

> **Live Roll — Circle your picks. Reveal the photographer's cut.**

An education-oriented alternative is:

> **Learn to see by editing alongside photographers you admire.**

## What the product is not

Live Roll should not launch as any of the following:

- A faster client-proofing gallery.
- A Capture One or Lightroom replacement.
- A camera-to-cloud infrastructure company.
- A wedding or event-photo delivery product.
- A shared guest camera roll.
- Another finished-photo feed.
- An AI culling product.
- A popularity-voting photo contest.
- A RAW storage service.
- A universal DSLR tethering app.

These boundaries are not temporary marketing language. They protect the only differentiated behavior found in research.

## Market findings

### Crowded live-delivery and proofing market

| Product | Existing capability | Strategic implication |
|---|---|---|
| [Capture One Live](https://support.captureone.com/hc/en-us/articles/4406763230481-Quick-Start-Guide-for-Capture-One-Live) | Tethered captures appear automatically in a browser; viewers follow new frames, rate, color-tag, and comment; edits update live | Near-exact private/client Roll, with a free session and a low paid price anchor |
| [Picflow](https://picflow.com/online-proofing) | Live watched-folder gallery, favorites, approve/reject, comments, drawing and literal circles/freehand annotations, selection limits | Even the tactile client-circle interaction is occupied |
| [Evoto Instant](https://www.evoto.ai/features/client-gallery) | Mobile tethering across major camera brands, live galleries, AI culling/editing, favorites, face search, payments, and tips | A mobile professional workflow an MVP cannot out-feature |
| [Frame.io Camera to Cloud](https://frame.io/features/c2c) | Supported cameras upload for near-real-time collaboration and review | Established camera-to-cloud infrastructure |
| [SpotMyPhotos](https://www.spotmyphotos.com/) | Fast camera-to-phone delivery, private matching, event/headshot workflows, retouching and sales | Event delivery is mature and high-service |
| [Honcho](https://thehoncho.app/) | Tethered live delivery, face search, live slideshows, and photo sales | Live event monetization is occupied |
| [PhotoShelter Real-Time](https://go.photoshelter.com/brands/real-time-workflow/) | FTP-to-DAM workflows, automated tags, and rapid stakeholder distribution | News, sports, and brand workflow is enterprise territory |
| Pixieset, ShootProof, Pic-Time | Branded client galleries, favorites, notes, downloads, and prints | Traditional proofing is mature |
| Camera-vendor transfer tools | FTP, captioning, rating, and field delivery | Capture transport continues to commoditize |

The conclusion is direct: progressive image arrival, remote ratings, circles, annotations, and client selects are prior art and commodity features. They are not a moat.

### Crowded photo-social market

VSCO, Glass, Foto, Flickr, 500px, and Irys provide finished-photo community, portfolio, discovery, or education. Irys explicitly positions itself as photography without video, ads, algorithms, AI, or visible follower counts. [Irys features](https://www.irysphotos.com/features)

The failure pattern is also relevant. Photo networks can launch with strong nostalgia or anti-Instagram positioning and still fail to create a durable graph. Lapse removed major social features after scale and storage pressure, and Poparazzi shut down despite millions of installs and venture funding. This makes “a better place for photographers to post” a poor thesis.

### Less-occupied selection-challenge market

No direct photography product was found that combines:

- A complete, bounded contact sheet from one authored shoot.
- A limited independent viewer edit.
- A locked photographer edit.
- A double-blind reveal.
- The photographer's finished crop, sequence, and explanation.
- A longitudinal private record of the viewer's taste relationship with photographers.

[Taste Trainer](https://www.self-referencing.org/about/taste-trainer) is the closest interaction found. It lets users rank images and compare results with experts or the community, but it is a generic art/design ranking tool rather than contact-sheet storytelling.

Lightroom Assisted Culling, Aftershoot, Photo Mechanic, and similar tools optimize a photographer's own workflow. GuruShots and ViewBug judge submitted finished images. AI critique apps evaluate individual photographs. None makes an expert's full sequence and independent editorial choice the media format.

This is real whitespace, but there is an important caveat: it may exist because licensing outtakes and persuading photographers to reveal rejected frames is difficult. The content supply—not the code—is likely the hardest asset.

## Recommended beachhead

Start with **serious photography hobbyists, students, and photographer-educators**.

Strong initial genres:

- Street photography.
- Documentary photography.
- Travel.
- Portraiture.
- Fashion/editorial.
- Music and controlled concert coverage.
- Architecture.

These genres benefit from interpreting timing, gesture, sequence, composition, and narrative. They also have educators who already explain process through YouTube, Patreon, workshops, books, and courses.

Do not initially target:

- Commercial studios and remote art directors.
- Weddings and general client proofing.
- High-volume sports/news desks.
- Shared event albums.
- Conflict, protest, medical, school, or child-focused photojournalism.
- General consumers with no active interest in photography.

The target user is not trying to deliver files faster. They want to develop visual judgment and understand how another photographer constructs a finished story.

## Core product model

### Roll

A bounded photographic sequence containing:

- A title and assignment.
- Photographer identity.
- Genre and context.
- A frame budget, normally 12, 24, or 36.
- A required select count.
- An ordered photographer cut.
- Final developed variants.
- Short selection notes or commentary.
- Public, unlisted, private, or classroom visibility.
- Rights, retention, and reuse rules.

### Frame

A single numbered image in capture order.

For the first release, a Frame needs:

- A compressed contact-sheet proxy.
- Optional selected/final developed image.
- Photographer note if selected.
- No exposed GPS or sensitive EXIF.

The app does not need RAW originals for archived challenges.

### Circle

A Circle is a private editorial selection, not a like.

- Each viewer receives a finite circle budget.
- Circles may be reassigned before locking.
- Other viewers' circles remain hidden.
- The photographer's selections remain hidden.
- A submitted cut becomes immutable for that attempt.

### Cut

An ordered set of selected Frames:

- `viewer_cut`
- `photographer_cut`
- `audience_cut`

The audience cut is descriptive, not authoritative. The photographer's cut preserves authorship.

### Develop

The reveal may show more than frame IDs:

- Final crop.
- Tonal/color development.
- Sequence position.
- Caption.
- One sentence or short audio note explaining why the photographer kept it.
- Optional explanation of a popular rejected frame.

Live Roll must not become a full photo editor. Development demonstrates the editorial result; it does not replace Lightroom.

## Core experiences

### Archived Blind Edit

This is the daily product and the recommended MVP center.

1. The user opens a Roll and reads the assignment.
2. They inspect 12–36 unmarked images in chronological order.
3. They receive a fixed number of grease-pencil-style circles.
4. They circle their keepers and optionally sequence a short essay.
5. They lock the cut.
6. The photographer's circles animate onto the contact sheet.
7. The app distinguishes matches, viewer-only, and photographer-only choices.
8. The user sees the developed final essay and photographer reasoning.
9. The result contributes to a private taste history.
10. The user continues to another Roll or follows the photographer.

The target session is three to six minutes.

### Live Roll premiere

Live uses the same decision ritual with additional uncertainty.

1. A photographer schedules a finite Roll and shares a poster/link.
2. Viewers join a lobby.
3. Numbered preview Frames progressively enter the fixed contact-sheet grid.
4. Viewers spend or move their limited Circles while future Frames remain unknown.
5. The photographer sees capture health and viewer count, never frame-level preferences.
6. The Roll seals.
7. The photographer independently locks a Cut and adds brief notes.
8. Viewers receive a reveal notification.
9. The double-blind comparison and final essay are revealed.
10. The Roll becomes an evergreen Blind Edit for later users.

A live Roll naturally creates two visits: the shoot and the reveal.

### Live broadcast modes

Later live capture can support:

- **Instant:** proxy appears after a short safety buffer.
- **Delayed:** proxy appears after a defined interval; recommended public default.
- **Reveal:** the sheet remains hidden until the Roll seals.
- **Curated:** the photographer or assistant chooses which captures are broadcast.

Modes must be labeled honestly. A curated Roll is not a complete contact sheet.

## Interaction and visual language

The product should borrow from physical editorial practice rather than ordinary social media.

- Fixed numbered contact-sheet grid.
- Grease-pencil circles drawn by long press or drag.
- Frame-count and circle-count constraints.
- Darkroom/development reveal animation.
- Contact sheet remains visible behind the finished essay as editorial evidence.
- No hearts, follower totals, star ratings, or public leaderboard.
- No comments or chat in MVP.
- No “correct answer” language.

Suggested reveal layers:

```text
Your Cut
Photographer's Cut
Audience Cut
Final Essay
```

Matching selections should feel satisfying, but disagreement is equally valuable when the photographer explains intent.

## Retention and growth loops

### Blind Edit habit

```text
Daily Roll
→ independent selection
→ photographer reveal
→ reasoning
→ next Roll
```

### Live premiere loop

```text
RSVP
→ live contact sheet
→ viewer locks Cut
→ photographer develops
→ reveal notification
→ comparison
→ next RSVP
```

### Taste history

Repeated Rolls may reveal private patterns without claiming objective quality:

- Greater affinity for gesture than geometry.
- Preference for close frames over environmental frames.
- Strong match with a particular photographer or genre.
- Changes in selection behavior over time.

The first release can store overlap history without generating speculative personality labels.

### Creator distribution

- Photographers share scheduled-Roll posters to existing audiences.
- Comparison cards link to the archived challenge.
- Final essays carry a subtle “Developed from 36 frames” card.
- Educators assign a Roll to students or cohorts.
- The app launches with a programmed slate rather than an empty public feed.

## Content seeding

The first useful release needs approximately:

- 20–30 excellent Rolls.
- 3–5 photographers minimum; 5–8 is preferable.
- Several genres and editing philosophies.
- Locked photographer cuts.
- Final developed selects.
- Concise reasoning.
- Explicit rights for every visible contact-sheet frame.

The founder's own photography archive can seed the prototype and reduce cold-start risk. The value of the first library matters more than the number of profiles.

A strong launch program would include:

> **One Block. Thirty-Six Frames. One Hour.**

Several photographers interpret the same bounded assignment and publish independent Rolls.

## Technical feasibility

### Archived-first MVP

The recommended archived challenge is technically low risk:

- Expo 57 iOS app.
- Static or server-backed web preview/share surface.
- Standard JPEG/WebP proxies.
- Database records for Rolls, Frames, Cuts, Circles, and reveals.
- RevenueCat SDK, RevenueCatUI Paywalls, and Customer Center.
- OneSignal or Expo push for daily and reveal notifications.

It does not require camera APIs, realtime infrastructure, background uploads, or external hardware.

### Live capture feasibility

The finite 24/36-frame format is feasible in Expo 57 when added:

1. `CameraView.takePictureAsync()` captures a temporary JPEG.
2. The app immediately copies it to a durable app directory.
3. SQLite records the local capture.
4. A small approximately 1280-pixel preview is generated.
5. Preview uploads first and becomes visible.
6. Original upload runs independently and resumes after interruption/relaunch.

Relevant versioned documentation: [Expo 57 Camera](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/camera/), [Expo 57 SQLite](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/sqlite/), and [Expo 57 FileSystem](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v57.0.0/sdk/filesystem-legacy/).

Do not promise:

- Burst or sustained sub-one-second capture.
- RAW, ProRAW, or universal HEIF capture through Expo Camera.
- Guaranteed upload completion after force-quit.
- Universal DSLR/mirrorless tethering.

Those require native AVFoundation work, vendor-specific SDKs, desktop companion software, or physical camera testing.

### Suggested live backend

If live premieres graduate into the product:

- Cloudflare Worker API.
- Durable Object per active Roll for authoritative state and hibernating WebSockets.
- D1 for accounts, catalog, subscription cache, and audit indices.
- R2 for immutable proxies and final variants.
- Direct presigned uploads to avoid proxying images through application servers.

At 36 frames, infrastructure cost is not the risk. Using 12 MB originals and 300 KB previews, one Roll is approximately 443 MB including originals. R2 storage is roughly fractions of a cent per retained Roll-month, while public preview egress is free under current R2 pricing. [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)

The harder risks are local capture safety, upstream speed, battery/thermal behavior, rights, and content supply.

## Double-blind integrity

The reveal must be enforced by server authorization, not hidden only in the UI.

Before the photographer locks a cut:

- Creator endpoints cannot read per-frame audience totals.
- Creator realtime events may show viewer count and completed-ballot count only.
- Viewers can read only their own circles.
- No public crowd totals exist.

The reveal transaction atomically:

- Freezes the photographer cut.
- Closes viewer edits.
- Snapshots crowd totals.
- Changes read permissions.
- Broadcasts the reveal state.

This independence is the core product promise.

## Safety, privacy, and rights

Contact sheets contain the photographs creators normally discard. That is central to the appeal and the largest supply-side risk.

Required rules:

- Photographer retains copyright.
- Hosting license is limited and explicit.
- Every visible frame—not only the final select—must be publishable.
- Strip GPS and sensitive EXIF from broadcast proxies.
- No precise live location.
- Public live mode defaults to a safety delay.
- Photographer can withdraw a frame during the safety window and abort a Roll.
- Watermarks are deterrence, not screenshot prevention.
- Public MVP prohibits minors and sexually explicit material.
- Provide report, block, removal, and account deletion.
- Avoid conflict/protest/medical use in initial marketing.
- Label substantial compositing or generative modification.
- Do not expose user-submitted public archives until moderation operations exist.

## Validation plan

### 1. Supply test

Ask 20 working photographers for one complete 24–36-frame sequence containing misses, plus the final developed cut and a short explanation.

**Proceed threshold:** at least 8 grant a 12-month app license.  
**Kill threshold:** fewer than 40% agree.

### 2. No-code challenge test

Create ten realistic clickable Blind Edits using founder-owned or licensed Rolls. Recruit 100 serious hobbyists or photography students.

Measure:

- Roll start.
- Frame inspection.
- Three or more selections.
- Cut lock.
- Reveal viewed.
- Next Roll started.

**Targets:** 60% complete one Roll, 40% spend the full selection budget, and median engaged session is at least six minutes.

### 3. Reasoning test

Randomize the reveal:

- Photographer selections only.
- Selections plus 60–90 seconds of reasoning.

**Target:** reasoning increases next-Roll starts by at least 20%.

### 4. Retention test

Publish three Rolls per week for four weeks.

**Conditional-go thresholds:** D7 at least 25%, D30 at least 12%, and 30% of users complete five Rolls.

### 5. Payment test

After three free completions, offer $4.99 monthly and $34.99–$39.99 annual.

**Targets:** at least 5% trial start and 35% trial-to-paid, or 20 hard annual preorders from the first 400 qualified users.

### 6. Creator repeat test

At least three of the first five participating educators must voluntarily publish and promote a second Roll.

### 7. Live incrementality test

Only after archived retention is visible, run five scheduled Live Rolls.

**Targets:** 30% RSVP-to-attendance, 12-minute median dwell, 40% return for reveal. Compare against the same creator using a YouTube/Instagram screen-share workflow.

## Product kill criteria

Kill or substantially change the concept if:

- Photographers will not license complete sequences.
- Fewer than 30–40% of qualified viewers submit a Cut.
- The developed reasoning does not improve continued play.
- Archived challenges perform no better than browsing finished photos.
- Photographers find commentary too burdensome.
- The product requires objective scoring to feel rewarding.
- The only validated demand is private proofing.

If only the Blind Edit retains users, remove live capture from the name and roadmap emphasis. If only client proofing works, treat it as a different company entering a heavily occupied market.

### Two-minute demo

1. Open **12 Frames at the Fair**.
2. Receive three grease-pencil Circles.
3. Inspect the complete unmarked Roll.
4. Circle an early frame.
5. Move the Circle to a stronger later frame.
6. Lock the Cut.
7. Animate the photographer's three Circles onto the sheet.
8. Show two matches and one meaningful disagreement.
9. Expand into the photographer's ordered developed essay.
10. Play the photographer's brief explanation.
11. Tap another Roll and show the RevenueCat Plus paywall.
12. End on the scheduled flagship Live Roll poster.

This demo proves the original interaction, learning value, visual craft, repeatable library, and monetization without pretending a social network already exists.

## Open questions for discussion

1. Does the founder have 10–20 complete sequences with rights to every frame and a clear final cut?
2. Is the emotional hook learning, comparison with a respected photographer, or the suspense of a live shoot?
3. Should the first library emphasize street/documentary work or commercially polished portrait/fashion work?
4. Is commentary best as one sentence per selected frame or a 60–90 second audio note?
5. Should the viewer select one decisive frame or sequence three to five frames into a story?
6. Is the eventual creator business subscription software, paid expert content, or educator cohorts?
7. Does the name need to retain “live” if synchronous Rolls remain a secondary format?

## Final recommendation

Preserve Live Roll as a project and validate the interaction before implementing the camera stack.

The original real-time gallery is a pass. The refined product is worth a serious prototype:

> **A contact-sheet challenge where you make the edit first, then discover the photographer's point of view.**

If people repeatedly complete that ritual and photographers willingly supply the material, live premieres become a differentiated expansion. If the ritual itself does not retain people, no amount of realtime camera engineering will rescue the idea.
